Ready to Send

Letters, requests and statements as plain text. Copy one, fill in the brackets, send it.

These go with Health: A User’s Guide, a book about American health care from the patient’s chair. A lot of what you’re entitled to never comes unless you ask in writing: an itemized bill, a hospital’s discount for people who can’t pay, the file behind an insurer’s no, your own chart. Asking pays off more often than you’d think. In one national survey, about three in four people who called about a bill they believed was wrong got it fixed. And of the Medicare Advantage denials in 2024, roughly one in nine was appealed, with about eight in ten of those appeals won at least in part.

How to send any of these. Swap each [bracket] for your own details, and delete the brackets. Use something that records the date: the plan’s or practice’s portal, email, or certified mail. Keep a copy, note the day you sent it, and set a reminder to follow up in two weeks. This isn’t legal advice. If a lot is riding on it, ask a legal-aid office to check the wording against your state’s rules.
Same jobs as the book, in different words. Each of these does the job of a letter, directive or request in the book’s appendices. The wording here was written for the web, so it won’t match the book line for line. Use whichever you prefer.

Records and bills

Your records

When and why You’re seeing a new doctor, getting a second opinion, appealing a denial, or just checking what your chart says. The office has thirty days to give you copies.
To The practice’s or hospital’s records department, which may be called health information management, or the request form in its portal.
How to send it However the office says it accepts requests, usually its portal or an email address it gives you.
Keep Your request and the date it went in. The thirty days start when the office receives it.
In the book Chapter 8, Your Records Are Yours.

Subject: Request for my medical records (HIPAA right of access)

To the records office:

Under 45 C.F.R. § 164.524, the HIPAA right of access, please send me all of the following for [start date] through [end date]:

- visit notes and test results
- imaging reports and the images themselves
- operative and pathology reports
- billing records

Format I'd like: [electronic file / patient portal / disc].

Please get me the copies within the thirty days the rule allows. If you charge for copies, tell me the fee before you make them.

[Name]
[Date of birth]
[Address]
[Phone]

The itemized bill

When and why A hospital bill showed up with only totals, and you want to see each line before paying.
To Patient financial services, as the hospital’s billing office is often called.
How to send it Through the portal or by email. You can call, but a call leaves no dated record.
Keep The date you asked, and the itemized bill once it arrives. Hold off on paying until then.
In the book Chapter 3, The Bill After, and the Debt.

Subject: Account [number]: itemized statement requested

To patient financial services:

I've received a bill for my care on [date]. Before any payment, I need to see the detail behind the totals, so please send me:

1. An itemized statement showing, for each charge, the CPT or HCPCS code, the revenue code, the quantity and the price.
2. Your financial assistance policy and its application form.

While I review these, please keep this account out of collections.

[Name]
[Account number]

Financial assistance

When and why Your income might qualify you for reduced-cost or free care. Once your application is complete, collection has to pause.
To The financial-assistance or financial-counseling office that the hospital’s policy names.
How to send it Follow the policy, which often means an upload or the mail. If you use email, ask them to confirm the application is complete.
Keep A copy of the application with everything you attached, and proof of when it arrived. The deadline is 240 days from the first bill.
In the book Chapter 3, The Bill After, and the Debt.

Subject: Financial assistance application, account [number]

My financial assistance application is attached. I have three requests:

1. Confirm you have it, and tell me if you need anything else.
2. Send me your decision in writing.
3. If I'm approved, refund whatever I've paid beyond the discounted amount.

As I understand it, extraordinary collection actions stop while a complete application is under review (26 C.F.R. § 1.501(r)-6). Note that on the account, please.

[Name]
[Account number]

A bill above the good-faith estimate

When and why You were paying for the care yourself, you got a written estimate, and the bill ran more than $400 over it.
To First the provider’s billing office. After that, the federal patient-provider dispute process.
How to send it Email or portal for the provider. The federal process has its own government form, due within 120 days of the bill.
Keep The estimate, the bill and both dates.
In the book Chapter 29, The Price Before.

Subject: Bill exceeds good-faith estimate by more than $400

Good-faith estimate you gave me: $[amount], dated [date], for [service].
Bill you sent me: $[amount], dated [date].

The difference is over $400. Please tell me why, and correct the bill to match the estimate.

If this isn't settled, I'll use the No Surprises Act's patient-provider dispute resolution process, which I can start up to 120 days after the date of the bill.

[Name]
[Account number]

Debt validation

When and why A collector has written to you about a medical debt that you don’t recognize or haven’t seen documented.
To The collection agency, using the address or email on its notice.
How to send it In writing, before the validation period on the notice runs out. If the agency takes consumer messages by email or on its website, those count. If not, use certified mail.
Keep The notice, your dispute and proof of the day you sent it.
In the book Chapter 3, The Bill After, and the Debt.

Subject: Dispute of debt, reference [number]

Your letter of [date], reference [number], says I owe this debt. I dispute it.

Please verify it in writing, as the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act provides. I want to see:

- who the original creditor is
- the dates of service
- the charges, itemized
- proof of your authority to collect it

Stop collecting until you've sent that. Separately, I'm applying to [hospital] for financial assistance.

[Optional:] Don't contact me at my job.

[Name]
[Address]

When the plan says no

An internal appeal

When and why Your plan turned down a treatment, test or claim.
To The appeals address on the denial letter.
How to send it Use the method the denial letter gives, whether mail, fax or portal, and email only if the letter offers it. Employer plans typically allow 180 days.
Keep A copy of the full packet, proof of delivery (a fax confirmation or tracking number), and your deadline on the calendar.
In the book Chapter 28, No: Who Decides, and How to Appeal.

Subject: Appeal of denial [reference number]

I'm appealing your [date] denial, reference [number], of [service] for [patient].

How the chart meets your criteria:

- Criterion: "[quote it]." Met by: [chart entry, date, page].
- Criterion: "[quote it]." Met by: [chart entry, date, page].

Attached is a letter of medical necessity from my physician.

Please have this reviewed by a physician who practices in [specialty]. If you haven't already sent me the full claim file and the criteria you relied on, send them now.

[Keep this paragraph only if it's urgent:] Waiting for a standard review would seriously jeopardize my health, so I'm asking for an expedited decision.

[Name]
[Member ID]

External review

When and why Your plan still says no after your internal appeal.
To Whichever office the final denial names: your state insurance department or the federal review process.
How to send it Within four months, on the form or website that office provides.
Keep A copy of all you sent, and the case number you get back.
In the book Chapter 28, No: Who Decides, and How to Appeal.

Subject: Request for independent external review, reference [number]

My plan upheld its denial in a final internal decision dated [date], reference [number]. I'm asking for an independent reviewer to look at it, through [my state's / the federal] external review process.

What the denial turned on: [medical necessity / treatment called experimental / rescission of my coverage].

Attached: the denial letters, my appeal, and the medical records that support it.

[Keep this paragraph only if it's urgent:] I need an expedited review. A delay would seriously jeopardize my health.

[Name]
[Member ID]
[Plan name]

A formulary exception

When and why A drug you need isn’t covered, sits on a high tier, or can’t be filled until you’ve tried something else.
To The unit at your plan that handles pharmacy benefits or coverage determinations, at the pharmacy number on your card, along with your prescriber’s statement.
How to send it The prescriber’s office usually files it by fax or portal. Medicare drug plans also have to accept the request by phone.
Keep The filing date, because the plan’s time to decide runs from then.
In the book Chapter 30, Drugs for Less.

Subject: Formulary exception request, [drug]

Please grant an exception and cover [drug, dose] [even though it isn't on your formulary / at a lower tier / without step therapy].

The attached statement from my prescriber explains why: the alternatives on your formulary [were tried and didn't work / would be less effective for me / would cause adverse effects].

I'd like your decision in writing.

[Keep this paragraph only if it's urgent:] A wait could seriously jeopardize my health, so please decide this on an expedited basis.

[Name]
[Member ID]

A complaint to a regulator

When and why You’ve written to a plan or provider twice and the problem still isn’t fixed.
To Whichever regulator covers it. For a plan: your state insurance department (see Who to Call), or the U.S. Department of Labor if it’s a self-funded employer plan. For a hospital: your state health department. For a doctor or nurse: the state licensing board.
How to send it Most regulators have an online complaint form. Paste this in or attach it.
Keep The complaint or case number.

Subject: Complaint against [plan / hospital / provider]

Who: [plan, hospital or provider], [member ID, license number or account number].

What happened: On [date], [two or three sentences].

What I've done about it: [I appealed / I wrote to them on (dates)]. The result: [what they said or did].

What I'm asking your office to do: [review the denial / investigate this practice / require them to respond].

The letters are attached. Please give me a case or complaint number.

[Name]
[Phone]

Getting seen

A network gap exception

When and why Nobody in your plan’s directory can see you within the plan’s own access standard.
To Member services or utilization review at the plan, with a copy to the regulator.
How to send it Portal or fax, with your call log attached.
Keep Your call log (dates, names, answers), plus dated screenshots of the directory.
In the book Chapter 32, Getting Seen.

Subject: Network gap exception request, [specialty]

This is a network gap exception request. For [specialty], your access standard is [X miles / X business days], and I can't get care within it.

From [date] to [date] I called [number] of the in-network [specialty] providers in your directory. None of them could see me within the standard. The attached log gives each call's date, phone number and answer.

[Name, credentials, address] can treat me and is willing to bill the plan. I'm asking you to approve out-of-network care with this provider, at my in-network cost sharing, and to confirm it in writing by [date].

Copy: [state insurance department / U.S. Department of Labor]

[Name]

Continuity of care

When and why You’re mid-treatment, and your doctor or hospital is about to leave the network.
To Your plan.
How to send it Portal or mail, as soon as you hear about the change.
Keep The plan’s written confirmation and the date the ninety days end.
In the book Chapter 32, Getting Seen.

Subject: Election of continuity of care, [provider name] leaving network

[Provider name], my [doctor / hospital], leaves your network on [date]. The No Surprises Act treats me as a continuing-care patient because I'm [pregnant / terminally ill / being treated for a serious and complex condition / scheduled for non-elective surgery].

I'm electing to keep seeing this provider at in-network cost sharing during the transition, which can run up to ninety days. Please confirm this in writing, with the end date.

[Name]
[Member ID]

Leaving the hospital

A discharge appeal, confirmed in writing

When and why You’re a Medicare inpatient, the hospital is sending you home, and you think it’s too soon.
To The quality-improvement organization listed on your Important Message from Medicare, with a copy to the hospital’s case manager.
How to send it Call the organization first, on the day of discharge at the latest; a phone request is valid. Then fax or email this letter to confirm.
Keep The time of the call, who you talked to, and the reference number.
In the book Chapter 20, Getting Out, and Getting Better at Home.

Subject: Confirming my request for discharge review, reference [number]

I'm writing to put on record that I contacted [quality-improvement organization] on [date] at [time] and asked for a review of my planned discharge from [hospital] on [date]. I made the request while still a patient there, under 42 C.F.R. § 405.1206.

The reference number is [number].

As I understand it, I can't be billed for the days the review is pending, apart from my usual cost sharing.

Copy: [hospital] case manager

[Name]
[Phone]

Before a planned procedure

Before a planned procedure: to the surgeon’s office

Send these weeks ahead, by email or portal, so you get the answers in writing. The surgeon’s office comes first, because its answers supply the names and codes for the other two.

Subject: Questions before my [procedure] on [date]

I'm scheduled for [procedure] on [date] at [facility]. Before then, I need to know:

1. Which CPT codes will you bill?
2. Will I be an inpatient or an outpatient?
3. Has prior authorization been approved? What's the reference number?
4. Who else will bill me? Please name any assistant surgeon, the anesthesia group, the pathology lab, and whoever supplies equipment I'll take home.

Send these weeks ahead, by email or portal, so you get the answers in writing. The surgeon’s office comes first, because its answers supply the names and codes for the other two.

Will any clinician with a part in this procedure, including anyone who reads a sample or an image, be out of network? Please tell me ahead of time.

Paying yourself: asking for one all-in price

To the surgeon’s office and the facility, before you book, if you have no insurance or will cover the whole bill yourself.

Subject: All-inclusive self-pay price for [procedure]

[Surgeon] would do my [procedure, CPT code if known], and I'd pay for it myself. I haven't scheduled it yet. Please send me these in writing first:

1. A single price covering the facility, the surgeon, anesthesia, [implants,] [pathology,] and [number] days of follow-up visits.
2. What's left out of that price, and what I'd pay for a complication or an overnight stay.
3. When payment is due, and whether I'd get it back if the procedure is canceled.
4. The good-faith estimate from each provider, which the law requires for self-pay patients.

I'll be comparing your answer with [another facility / my plan's allowed amount].

[Name]
[Phone]

Before a planned procedure: to HR

To HR or whoever administers benefits, before you approach the hospital.

Subject: Benefits question before planned surgery

I'm planning [procedure] in [month]. Before I talk to the hospital about cost, I have a question about our plan: does it offer a bundled-price program, a travel-surgery or center-of-excellence benefit, or case management for planned surgery?

If so, please send the terms: which hospitals, what I'd pay, what travel it covers for me and a companion, and how it handles follow-up care and complications once I'm home.

If not, is our plan self-funded? And at [price], would the plan consider a single-case agreement with [facility]?

[Name]

procedure-employer.txt

Your employer’s plan

Questions for your employer’s benefits office

By email to a union trustee, a benefits committee or HR. Anyone can ask the first three. The last three are about what the plan pays.

1. Does the company pay our claims itself (self-funded), or does it buy insurance (fully insured)? Who's the plan administrator, and who's the named fiduciary? May I have the full plan document rather than the summary?

2. For planned surgery or a serious diagnosis, does the plan have a case manager, a second-opinion service, or a center-of-excellence program?

3. When the administrator denies a claim, has the company ever stepped in to review it? Who would I write to?

4. A 2021 law requires administrators to provide claims data. Have we asked for ours? As a percentage of Medicare rates, what do our largest hospitals get paid?

5. For our ten most-used generic drugs, what does our pharmacy-benefit manager pay, and what would each cost at a pharmacy without insurance?

6. What's our broker or consultant paid, and who pays it? The same 2021 law requires them to tell us.

If you can’t speak for yourself

Advance directive: Everything

For someone who wants every treatment that might keep them alive. Start from your state’s official advance-directive form. Attach this page as your statement of wishes, or write it into the space the form leaves for your own wishes. Follow your state’s signing rules for witnesses or a notary. Give copies to your agent (also called a proxy) and alternate, your regular doctors, and the hospital you’d most likely go to, and keep a list of who has one.

MY WISHES FOR MY CARE

1. Who speaks for me
I appoint as my health-care agent: [name, relationship, phone]. If [he / she / they] can't serve, I appoint as alternate: [name, phone].

2. The care I want
If I can't speak for myself, keep me alive. I want dialysis, a breathing machine, resuscitation, artificial nutrition and hydration, and any other treatment that may prolong my life, for as long as my doctors think it can extend my life. [Any exceptions.]

3. When treatment may end
Continue until my doctors agree that treatment can no longer prolong my life, [and my agent agrees / and my agent has had a second opinion before anything is withdrawn].

4. Comfort
Treat my pain and distress, alongside everything else.

5. What matters to me
[My faith and community, any clergy or tradition to consult, and where I want to be.]

6. If this doesn't cover the question
To my agent: pick whichever course is more likely to keep me alive.

Advance directive: Comfort First

For someone who wants treatment aimed at recovery, and comfort if recovery is out of reach. Start from your state’s official advance-directive form. Attach this page as your statement of wishes, or write it into the space the form leaves for your own wishes. Follow your state’s signing rules for witnesses or a notary. Give copies to your agent (also called a proxy) and alternate, your regular doctors, and the hospital you’d most likely go to, and keep a list of who has one.

MY WISHES FOR MY CARE

1. Who speaks for me
I appoint as my health-care agent: [name, relationship, phone]. I appoint as alternate: [name, phone].

2. What recovery means to me
Give me treatment that's likely to get me back to a life where I can [recognize the people I love / talk with them / live somewhere other than a hospital or nursing facility / something else, in my words].

3. If nobody can tell
When it isn't clear whether treatment will get me back there, try it for [so many days or weeks, or "what my doctors consider a fair trial"]. Then review it with my agent.

4. If it looks unlikely
Once my doctors believe I probably won't get back to that life, I don't want [hospital admission / resuscitation / dialysis / a breathing machine / artificial nutrition]. Focus my care on comfort, through hospice or palliative care, [wherever I am / at home].

5. Comfort
Treat my pain and distress fully, even if that could shorten my life.

6. What I care about
[Who I want with me. Music, faith, place. What I'd want to avoid.]

7. If this doesn't cover the question
To my agent: decide the way you think I would. Whatever you decide on that basis, the responsibility is mine, not yours.

Advance directive: Enough

For someone who doesn’t want their life prolonged past a line they set in advance. Start from your state’s official advance-directive form. Attach this page as your statement of wishes, or write it into the space the form leaves for your own wishes. Follow your state’s signing rules for witnesses or a notary. Give copies to your agent (also called a proxy) and alternate, your regular doctors, and the hospital you’d most likely go to, and keep a list of who has one.

MY WISHES FOR MY CARE

1. Who speaks for me
I appoint as my health-care agent: [name, relationship, phone]. I appoint as alternate: [name, phone].

2. My line
Stop prolonging my life once my doctor and my agent agree that I probably will never again [recognize the people I love / carry on a conversation / follow a story or read / live somewhere other than a nursing facility / some other sign, in my words].

3. What I refuse past that line
- surgery
- dialysis
- a breathing machine
- resuscitation
- artificial nutrition and hydration
- antibiotics or other treatment for an infection such as pneumonia
- transfer to a hospital, unless nothing else can relieve my suffering

4. Food and drink
[Offer me food and drink only when I show I want them, for comfort, and never press them on me. / Don't feed me by hand.] If I open my mouth when food is offered, that doesn't mean I've agreed to be kept alive by it.

5. Comfort
Treat my pain, breathlessness and distress fully, with hospice care, even if that could shorten my life.

6. What a life I'd enjoy means to me
[My own description, written now while I'm able.]

7. If I seem content past the line
To my agent: [follow this document regardless; I knew this could happen when I wrote it / count my contentment, and decide as you think best].

Advance directive: My People Decide

For someone who’d rather trust a person than a form. Start from your state’s official advance-directive form. Attach this page as your statement of wishes, or write it into the space the form leaves for your own wishes. Follow your state’s signing rules for witnesses or a notary. Give copies to your agent (also called a proxy) and alternate, your regular doctors, and the hospital you’d most likely go to, and keep a list of who has one.

MY WISHES FOR MY CARE

1. Who speaks for me
I appoint as my health-care agent: [name, relationship, phone]. I appoint as alternate: [name, phone]. Follow my agent's decisions even when anyone in my family objects.

2. What my agent can do
My agent may make every health-care decision that I could make myself. That covers starting, refusing or stopping any treatment, artificial nutrition and hydration among them. [My agent may also see my medical records and talk with all my clinicians.]

3. What I've said
My agent and I have talked about what matters to me. The short version: [a few sentences of my own].

4. Comfort
Whatever else is decided, treat my pain and distress.

5. To my agent
Decide what you believe I'd decide. If you can't tell, choose what you think is best for me. I picked you for this, and whatever you choose, I'm grateful.
