You're going to help me get my health care organized. Take the seven jobs below one at a time, in order.

Ground rules:
- Get my OK before you send anything or add anything to my calendar.
- Don't guess at any health fact. Ask me, or write [unknown].
- You aren't my doctor. That means no diagnosing, and no advice to begin, end or alter a treatment.
- If something I say sounds urgent, stop and send me to my doctor or 911.

Job 1: Build my health file. Ask me a few questions at a time until you have:
- my conditions, with the year each was diagnosed
- my medicines and supplements, with the dose and prescriber
- my allergies, and what each one did to me
- my pharmacies, doctors, dentists and therapists, with their phone numbers and portals
- the hospital I'd pick
- my insurance: plan name and type, administrator, member ID, plan year, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, and the location of the plan documents
- who my health-care proxy is, and whether I've signed an advance directive and an authorization
- recent test results worth keeping
Then turn my answers into a one-page health file.

Job 2: Make it findable from any phone. Save each of these as an email draft to myself. Start the subject line with its tag, which nobody else would use, and repeat the tag in the body so one search pulls it up:
- health-info: the health file
- rx-info: medicines, with start date, dose, prescriber and pharmacy
- ins-info: insurance details and the plan's contact information
- care-team: doctors and portals
- records-log: a running list of each request I've sent and when its answer is due
When something changes, don't edit the old draft. Save a new one with the date in the subject.

Job 3: Request my records. Draft a request to every doctor and hospital on my list for my complete records in electronic form, citing 45 C.F.R. § 164.524 (the HIPAA right of access) and its thirty-day deadline. Draft another to my health plan for [three] years of claims history. Show me every draft first. Log each request in records-log along with the date its answer is due, and add a calendar follow-up for that date.

Job 4: Check my network. Using my plan's name and type, point me to its provider directory. Then give me a short script for calling each office to ask whether it's in my plan's network this year and accepting new patients. Trust the call over the directory, since directories are often wrong, and record the answer in care-team with the date and who I talked to.

Job 5: Look for conflicts of interest. If you can search the web, look in Open Payments, the federal database (openpaymentsdata.cms.gov), for each doctor who's proposed a drug, device, test or operation for me. Report any payments by company, type and year, linking to each. Flag any public record showing that a doctor has a stake in a facility, lab or imaging center they've sent me to. "No record found" doesn't mean "no interest," so keep those apart, and don't draw conclusions about the doctor.

Job 6: Keep it current. Add these to my calendar as recurring events, each with a one-line note on its purpose:
- every January: pull my results from each portal into health-info
- any time a medicine changes: update rx-info that day
- before each open enrollment: recheck the network for everyone on care-team, and price my prescriptions
- every birthday: confirm the directive and proxy are still current, and that my proxy knows how to find health-info

Job 7: Report back. List what you made, what's still waiting on someone else and its due date, and each assumption I should check.
